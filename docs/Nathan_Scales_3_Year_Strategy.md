# Nathan Scales — 3-Year Growth Strategy
### Adapted from the Brezscales Playbook

Brez's mechanics were built for a paid-ads/rev-share agency selling to e-commerce brands with an aspirational US/consumer audience. Nathan Scales sells websites, lead-capture systems, and custom software — mostly to B2B/local service businesses (construction, roofing, etc.). Same underlying architecture (proof-first → content → productized recurring revenue), different delivery mechanics. This doc translates the structure, not the specifics.

---

## Mechanic Translation Table

| Brez mechanic | Nathan Scales equivalent |
|---|---|
| Rev-share on ad spend (10–20% of revenue generated) | Performance-linked pricing: reduced/deferred build fee + a finder's fee (e.g. 3–8%) on any contract closed through the lead-capture system in the first 90 days. Fits high-ticket B2B niches like construction well. |
| "$10 in, $50 out" proof number | One sharp before/after metric once the first client is live — e.g. "leads responded to in under 2 hours vs. their old 3-day average." |

| Brandlab vs. @brezscales (brand split) | Decide: does "Nathan Scales" stay one brand, or split into Nathan Tendo (personal/build-in-public) + Nathan Scales (clean agency storefront)? |
| Clipper army | Skip until Year 2+, once there's an audience worth distributing. |

### Q1: Land the first client
Per the Brez model, everything else waits on this. He landed his first clients from his existing network with a "pay only if I perform" offer — not from an audience or content.
- Reply to any current responses; push Touch 2–4 on the remaining Kampala/Wakiso leads.

**Year 1 exit targets:** 3–5 paying clients · one repeatable proof metric · lead qualification automated · brand-split decision made.

## Year 2 — Content Engine & Productized Offers (Months 13–24)

### Q1: Turn results into content
- Ramp the carousel/short-form series using real client outcomes as the hook, not hypotheticals.
- If you split brands, this is where the personal account starts feeding the agency account — the same relationship @brezscales has to Brandlab.

### Q2: Build the funnel, add a low-ticket entry point
- Free lead magnet: a short case-study or mini-guide ("how we cut lead response time by X%"), gated behind an email/WhatsApp capture — your version of the free webinar, fitted to a service business instead of a course sale.
- A fixed-price productized package (your version of the $47–97 bootcamp) — a flat-fee starter system for businesses not ready for a full custom build. Gets you volume and more proof points faster than only chasing bespoke high-ticket work.

### Q3–Q4: Scale client base, formalize the retainer tier
- Target 8–15 active clients across niches.
- For proven, high-trust clients, introduce a "growth partner" retainer: ongoing lead pipeline management + reporting, priced above the one-time build.

**Year 2 exit targets:** working content funnel · repeatable productized offer · 8–15 clients · combined revenue scaled to your actual market (not Brez's US/e-com pricing).

## Year 3 — Platform & Recurring Revenue (Months 25–36)

### Q1: Productize Jarvis
This is your AdLevel move. Package Jarvis — or a lighter client-facing version — as a subscription tool baked into delivery: clients (or other small agencies) pay monthly to run lead review, follow-up automation, and reporting through it. Bundle it into the retainer tier rather than selling it as optional — that's what makes it recurring instead of incidental.

### Q2: Free front-end, gated backend
- Shift your entry offer to a fully free walkthrough with wider distribution.
- Move higher-tier offers behind an application/discovery call instead of a public price list — more pricing flexibility as demand grows.

### Q3–Q4: Full ecosystem, brand separation locked in
Four pieces working together, each protecting the others: personal brand (build-in-public) → agency brand (client work) → productized offer (starter package) → SaaS layer (Jarvis). Bring on contractors or a small team so you're not the bottleneck on every build.

**Year 3 exit targets:** recurring SaaS/retainer revenue overtakes one-off build fees as the largest line · multiple niches served off one repeatable system · Jarvis generating revenue independent of your time.

## Caveats before you copy this

- **Brez's rev-share works because ad spend produces directly attributable revenue in real time.** Your systems (websites, lead capture, software) produce leads and efficiency gains — one or two steps removed from revenue. Make your performance-pricing terms watertight about what counts as "a result" (a qualified lead? a signed contract?) or you'll end up in disputes.
- **Brez's audience is consumer/aspirational** (young people who want to start an agency). Your buyers are construction and service-business owners — decision-makers who respond to turnaround time, responsiveness, and revenue attribution, not lifestyle content or follower counts. Translate "proof" accordingly.
- **Treat his self-reported revenue figures as directional inspiration, not a scorecard** — they're unverified and denominated in a completely different market than yours.
